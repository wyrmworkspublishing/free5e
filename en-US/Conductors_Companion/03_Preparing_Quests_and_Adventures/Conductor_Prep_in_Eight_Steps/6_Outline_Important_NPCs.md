### 6. Outline Important NPCs

During our preparation, we'll outline those NPCs (nonplayer characters) most critical to the adventure, focusing on a name and a connection to the adventure.
Many other NPCs — maybe even most of them — can be improvised right at the table.

#### NPC Generator

NPCs bring the setting to life.
Use these tables to build NPCs quickly, then add voice, priorities, and roleplaying detail as needed.
For major NPCs, choose or roll on most steps.
For minor NPCs, one physical detail, one motivation, and one behavior cue are usually enough.
Ancestry adds context, not destiny.
Worldview guides decisions; behavior shows how those decisions appear.

> **NPC Name Generator** \

> 4. Combine, tweak, and you’re done.

##### Step 3: Disability, Difference, and Adaptation

Disabled NPCs belong in ordinary life, positions of authority, and adventure.
Do not reduce them to one trait or frame them as a lesson, pity object, or problem to be fixed.

Decide what accessibility looks like in your setting: ramps, interpreters, tactile signage, adapted tools, mobility magic, or uneven access from place to place.
Some groups do not want disability-based prejudice in play; others may explore it deliberately.
Set expectations together.

Each result below describes presentation, not diagnosis.
Describe what characters notice, the NPC's routines, and the support that helps them function.

|  d100  | Trait                         | Presentation                                                   | Adaptations                                           | Support                                                      |
|:------:|:------------------------------|:---------------------------------------------------------------|:------------------------------------------------------|:-------------------------------------------------------------|
| 01–14  | Mobility difference           | Moves differently than expected; walking, balance, climbing, or standing is altered | Route-finding; efficient movement; practiced technique | Mobility aids, mounts, ramps/lifts, time to navigate         |
| 15–24  | Chronic pain or fatigue       | Pain or exhaustion managed as part of routine                  | Pacing; persistence; risk assessment                  | Rest breaks, pain care, lighter loads, flexible timing       |
| 25–32  | Vision difference             | Limited, altered, or non-visual perception                     | Auditory/tactile mapping; memory of spaces            | Tactile maps, service animal, cane, clear verbal descriptions |
| 33–38  | Hearing difference            | Reduced or absent auditory input                               | Visual attention; reading body language               | Sign language, written notes, visual signals                 |
| 39–44  | Sensory processing difference | Heightened or reduced response to sound, light, texture, or smell | Pattern detection; focus in tuned environments        | Quiet/dim space, warnings before chaos, sensory tools        |
| 45–54  | Neurodivergent cognition      | Attention, learning, social inference, or inner experience differs | Lateral thinking; specialization; directness          | Clear expectations, written info, routines, patience         |

##### Step 4: Worldview

Worldview describes how an NPC reads other people, judges risk, and decides what matters.
Start with a baseline DC 12 for social checks.
Roll or choose one worldview.
If the approach aligns with what the NPC values, lower the DC by the listed amount.
If it presses on what they distrust or fear, raise the DC by that amount.

| d6 | View of Others | View of Risk | Core Motivation | Modifier |
|:--:|:---------------|:-------------|:----------------|:--------:|
| 1  | Distrustful    | Avoidant     | Security        |   ±3     |
| 2  | Guarded        | Cautious     | Stability       |   ±2     |
| 3  | Neutral        | Calculated   | Advantage       |   ±1     |
| 4  | Open           | Willing      | Growth          |   ±1     |
| 5  | Welcoming      | Bold         | Belonging       |   ±2     |
| 6  | Idealizing     | Reckless     | Impact          |   ±3     |

**Example:** **Guarded / Cautious / Stability / ±2** resists rushed demands, prefers clear plans, and responds best to promises of order and continuity.

##### Step 5: Social Role

Social role defines what an NPC does, where they do it, and why others make room for them.
It does not determine class, level, or combat ability.
Roll once on the table or choose a row.

| d12 | Work Done                   | Social Sphere              | Why They Belong |
|:---:|:----------------------------|:---------------------------|:----------------|
|  1  | Food or raw goods           | Land or households         | They own it     |
|  2  | Herding, hunting, or gathering | Frontier or wild routes | They know the ground |
|  3  | Crafted goods               | Trades or guilds           | They're skilled |
|  4  | Trade or transport          | Markets or roads           | They're relied on |
|  5  | Service or care             | Inns, homes, or clinics    | People trust them |
|  6  | Records or law              | Courts or offices          | They hold office |
|  7  | Rites or religion           | Temples or shrines         | Tradition backs them |
|  8  | Guarding or fighting        | Watch, militia, or army    | They hold rank |
|  9  | Performance or art          | Taverns, stages, or festivals | They entertain |

| 12  | Guiding, fixing, or brokering | Roads, camps, or crossroads | They get results |

**Example:** **Crafted goods / Trades or guilds / They're skilled** implies a blacksmith, artisan, or apothecary.

##### NPC Example

**Results:** **Name:** Vei sworn to Toren.
**Ancestry:** Underlands → Dwarf.
**Physical:** Hands — ink-stained fingers, callused palms, missing finger.
**Worldview:** Guarded / Cautious / Stability / ±2.
**Social Role:** Records or law / Courts or offices / They hold office.
**Behavior:** Reserved / Formal / Deliberate / Precise.

Vei serves as a court recordkeeper trusted to maintain continuity during transitions.
They resist rushed demands, favor clear procedures, and respond best to orderly, well-reasoned requests grounded in precedent.
