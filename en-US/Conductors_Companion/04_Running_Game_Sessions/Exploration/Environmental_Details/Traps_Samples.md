#### Traps in Play
When adventurers come across a trap, you need to know how the trap is triggered and what it does, as well as the possibility for the characters to detect the trap and to disable or avoid it.

##### Triggering a Trap

Most traps are triggered when a creature goes somewhere or touches something that the trap’s creator wanted to protect.
Common triggers include stepping on a pressure plate or a false section of floor, pulling a trip wire, turning a doorknob, and using the wrong key in a lock.
Magic traps are often set to go off when a creature enters an area or touches an object.
Some magic traps (such as the _Glyph of Warding_ spell) have more complicated trigger conditions, including a password that prevents the trap from activating.

##### Detecting and Disabling a Trap

Characters can usually detect traps through careful inspection.
They might notice an uneven flagstone concealing a pressure plate, a gleam of light off a trip wire, or small holes in the walls that will erupt in flames.
A trap’s description specifies the checks and DCs needed to detect, disable, or both.
A character actively looking for a trap can attempt a Wisdom (Perception) check against the trap’s DC.
Alternatively, compare the DC to each character’s passive Wisdom (Perception) score to determine if anyone notices the trap in passing.
If detected before triggering, characters can disarm it permanently or long enough to move past it.
An Intelligence (Investigation) check can be used to deduce what needs to be done, followed by a Dexterity check using thieves’ tools to sabotage it.
Any character can attempt an Intelligence (Arcana) check to detect or disarm a magic trap, in addition to other checks noted in the trap’s description.
The DCs are the same regardless of the check.
Dispel Magic has a chance of disabling most magic traps.
A magic trap’s description provides the DC for the ability check when using Dispel Magic.
In most cases, a trap’s description is clear enough to adjudicate whether a character’s actions locate or foil it.
Avoid die rolling to override clever play and good planning.
Use your common sense and the trap’s description to determine the outcome.
No trap’s design can anticipate every possible action.
Allow a character to discover a trap without an ability check if an action clearly reveals its presence.
For instance, if a character lifts a rug that hides a pressure plate, they’ve found the trigger and no check is needed.
Foiling traps can be trickier.
Consider a trapped treasure chest.
If opened without pulling the handles, a mechanism fires poison needles at anyone in front.
After inspecting and making checks, the characters are still unsure.
Instead of opening it, they prop a shield and push the chest open with an iron rod.
The trap triggers, but the needles harmlessly hit the shield.
Traps often have mechanisms to disarm or bypass them.
Intelligent monsters in their lairs need ways to pass them without harming themselves.
Such traps might have hidden levers that disable triggers or secret doors that bypass them.

##### Trap Effects

Traps can range from inconvenient to deadly, using elements like arrows, spikes, blades, poison, toxic gas, fire, and pits.
Deadly traps combine multiple elements to kill, injure, contain, or drive off creatures.
Trap descriptions specify their triggers.
Attack bonuses, save DCs, and damage vary by severity.
Use the Trap Difficulty and Damage by Level table for suggestions based on three levels of severity.
**Setback** traps won’t kill or seriously harm characters of the indicated levels.
**Dangerous** traps will seriously injure (and potentially kill) them.
**Deadly** traps will likely kill them.

##### Complex Traps

Complex traps, like standard traps, execute a series of actions each round once activated.
They turn dealing with traps into a combat encounter.
When activated, complex traps roll initiative and include an initiative bonus.
On their turn, they activate again, often taking actions like making successive attacks, creating a time-changing effect, or producing a dynamic challenge.
Otherwise, they can be detected and disabled or bypassed as usual.
For instance, a trap that slowly floods a room works best as a complex trap.
On its turn, the water level rises, and after several rounds, the room is completely flooded.
