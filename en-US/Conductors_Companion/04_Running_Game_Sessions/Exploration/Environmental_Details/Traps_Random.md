#### Random Traps
Traps add tension and consequence to exploration when they make sense for the environment and can be discovered or avoided through clever play rather than chance alone.
Think of them as environmental storytelling—signs of danger, ancient defenses, or magical remnants of forgotten civilizations.

When adding traps, consider:

* What purpose did the trap originally serve?  
* What clues might reveal its presence?  
* How can its discovery or activation change the situation?

Use the table below to pair a physical or narrative trigger with a trap or hazard appropriate to the party’s tier.
Roll 1d20 for a trigger and again for the matching tier column, or select results that fit your adventure.
Adjust effects, DCs, and damage to match the characters’ level and the environment.

Each result in the table provides a theme — interpret it to fit the location.
For example, Force Beams might represent a magical defense turret in a ruin, while Falling Beam could be a collapsing roof in a barn.
Combine traps with the setting and story stakes: a radiant ward protecting a relic, molten vents in a volcanic chamber, or a cursed idol radiating psychic backlash.
Even simple hazards become memorable when they make sense within the world and affect player choices.
