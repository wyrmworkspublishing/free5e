### Designing a Dungeon
Start small—a single chamber, corridor, or door—and expand outward as players explore.

## 1. Review the Characters

Identify what skills, spells, and senses the party relies on for exploration and survival.
Adjust obstacles and descriptions accordingly so each character’s abilities influence how the dungeon unfolds.

## 2. Create a Strong Start

Open with action or urgency—a call for rescue, a collapsing entryway, a guardian attack, or news of a recent expedition gone wrong.
The first moments should establish tone and motive for the crawl.

## 3. Outline Potential Scenes

Plan three key beats to define the adventure:

1. **Conflict:** a trap triggered by the narrative or guardians defending their domain.  
2. **Discovery:** hidden lore, treasure, or the reason the dungeon still exists.  
3. **Revelation:** what happens when the party disturbs the balance within.

## 4. Define Secrets and Clues

Every dungeon has history.
Scatter evidence of it through objects, writing, or remains:

4. Wall carvings or murals that show the builder’s purpose.  
5. Journals or inscriptions that hint at prior expeditions.  
6. Objects out of place—proof of intrusion or corruption.

## 5. Consider the Dungeon’s Purpose

Ask how this place came to be and how it endures.
Was it a fortress, tomb, or experiment?
What power sustains it now?
The creatures within should make sense—were they summoned, trapped, or native to the structure?
Why do they remain, and what do they want?
A believable ecosystem doesn’t need complexity—just internal logic.

## 7. Populate the Dungeon

Not every inhabitant is an enemy.
Some are prisoners, caretakers, scavengers, or explorers who arrived earlier.
Ask:

7. How did they get here?  
8. Why do they stay?  
9. What were they doing before the party arrived?  
Their answers shape both encounter tone and pacing.

## 8. Choose Relevant Monsters

Select monsters that fit the dungeon’s origin and current state: undead in crypts, oozes in decay, constructs in laboratories, zealots in temples.

## 9. Select Rewards

Reward exploration with story and meaning as well as treasure.
Uncovering _why_ the dungeon exists may be as valuable as what it holds.
Use _Rewards_ in _Conducting Game Sessions_ to balance loot.
