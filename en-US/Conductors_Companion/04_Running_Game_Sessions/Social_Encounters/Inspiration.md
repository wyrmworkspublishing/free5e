## Inspiration
**Note:** Don't use Inspiration with the **Variant Rule: Luck** (Chapter XX, Player's Guide).
Inspiration is a tool for Conductors to reward players who bring their characters to life by aligning their actions with personality traits and values.
It emphasizes roleplaying and storytelling, giving players a tangible benefit for investing in their character's narrative.
For example, a character driven by compassion might negotiate with a Beggar Prince, or one bonded to their home village could resist a charm spell through sheer determination.

### Awarding Inspiration
As the Conductor, you can grant inspiration to players for portraying their characters compellingly.
This includes leaning into personality traits, grappling with flaws, or advancing bonds and ideals in ways that deepen the story.
You should communicate how players can earn inspiration in your game, tailoring it to your group's style.
Award inspiration to each character at the beginning of a session.
This takes some of the weight off of needing to remember to reward inspiration during the game.
You can still award it again during the game if players have used it.

### Using Inspiration
Players can spend inspiration to gain advantage on an attack roll, saving throw, or ability check.
A player can only have one instance of inspiration at a time—this resource is not stackable.
You can encourage players to use their inspiration strategically and to reward each other for standout moments in roleplay, clever tactics, or thrilling contributions to the game.
By fostering a culture of mutual recognition, inspiration becomes a dynamic way to enhance storytelling and player engagement.
