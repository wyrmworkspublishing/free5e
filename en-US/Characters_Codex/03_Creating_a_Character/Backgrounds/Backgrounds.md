# Backgrounds

Every story begins somewhere.
Your character’s background is about what they did before becoming an adventurer.
It shapes their origins, path to adventuring, and role in the world.
A Fighter might be a knight or soldier, a Wizard might be a sage or artisan, or a Rogue might be a thief or jester.
While cultures reflect the environment and values of the community that your character considers home, backgrounds reflect your character’s individual experiences before adventuring.

Their background offers vital story cues: what changed to drive them to adventure, why they left their past life, how they obtained their gear, or why they lack wealth despite family riches.

**📌 NOTE**\
If a chosen background grants a proficiency your character already has from another source, you may select a different proficiency of the same type (skill, tool, or language).

## Creating a Background

Consider how your character’s background connects to their class, skills, experiences, and distinctive traits.

### Skill Proficiencies

Choose two skills your character did not gain from their class. Your character gains proficiency in those skills.

### Tool Proficiencies and/or Languages

Choose one of the following combinations to reflect what your character learned before adventuring:

* One tool proficiency and one language
* Two tool proficiencies
* Two languages

Choose proficiencies that fit the background’s concept. For example, a sailor might know _Athletics_ and _Acrobatics_, along with _Water Vehicles_ and _Navigator’s Tools_.

_Ability Scores Commonly Associated with Tool and Vehicle Proficiencies_

| Ability | Commonly Associated Tool or Vehicle Proficiencies |
| --: | :-- |
| Strength | Artisan Tools (by type) |
| Dexterity | Artisan Tools (by type), Land Vehicles, Musical Instruments (by type), Thieves’ Tools |
| Intelligence | Artisan Tools (by type), Disguise Kit, Forgery Kit, Gaming Set, Navigator’s Tools, Poisoner’s Kit, Water Vehicles |
| Wisdom | Herbalism Kit |
| Charisma | Musical Instruments |

### Starting Equipment

Choose about 30 gp of equipment and money that fits the background, usually about half in gear and half in coin. You may exchange items for equal-value equipment or gold with your Conductor’s approval.

### Feature

Create a narrative-focused feature that helps the character interact with the world through contacts, specialized knowledge, reputation, access, or similar story opportunities. Broader access should generally provide less specific or reliable benefits.

#### Contacts

* **Local Expert.** Knowledgeable about a particular region or community and its customs and people.
* **Guild Connection.** Access to a guild’s contacts, resources, information, or occasional favors.
* **Military Rank.** Recognition within a military or similar organization that may provide aid or information.
* **Friendly Rival.** A former competitor who can occasionally provide assistance, information, or advice.

#### Social Awareness

* **Streetwise.** You know how to gather rumors and locate useful people in urban communities.
* **Charitable Soul.** You can seek modest assistance from charitable, religious, or mutual-aid communities.
* **Cultural Fluency.** You have expertise on ability checks made to follow or interpret the social protocols of one region, community, or social group.
* **Traveling Trader.** You understand merchants, local economies, trade routes, and sources of unusual goods.
* **Small-Town Hero.** You are readily trusted and recognized in rural communities similar to your own.

#### Knowledge & Wisdom

* **Mentor’s Guidance.** You can contact a former mentor who may provide information or advice.
* **Wilderness Familiarity.** You can more easily locate food and shelter in one specific region or environment.
* **Career Skill.** You have experience in a trade and gain benefits on checks directly related to that profession.

The Acolyte example shows how a background combines features, proficiencies, and roleplaying details.
One way to create your own is to modify the acolyte or adapt an existing entry from the table below—choose fitting skills, tools or languages, and a feature, then describe how those experiences led to adventuring.
